# The Diátaxis compass

Use the compass when intuition does not clearly identify the required document, when existing material feels difficult to write or use, or when its declared type conflicts with what its sentences actually do.

## Interpret the terms

The skill’s classification table makes two independent decisions. Read its terms as:

- **Action:** practical steps, decisions, or doing.
- **Cognition:** theoretical or propositional knowledge, facts, or reflection.
- **Acquisition:** study—the reader is gaining skill or understanding.
- **Application:** work—the reader is applying existing competence.

Use the terms flexibly enough to expose the reader’s actual need; do not let uncertainty about a label replace the decision.

## Apply it in both directions

Use the compass to classify:

- **a user situation:** What does this reader need now?
- **intended content:** What am I trying to help the reader do or know?
- **existing content:** What need does this sentence, section, or page actually serve?

Ask the questions from different perspectives when needed:

- Am I writing for action or cognition?
- Is the material in front of me serving acquisition or application?
- Does the user need to study or work?
- Do I want the reader to do something or understand something?

Apply the compass at multiple scales. A title may claim one quadrant while individual paragraphs serve another. Inspect words, sentences, sections, and the whole document.

## Treat difficulty as a signal

Confusion, digression, and resistance often indicate crossed boundaries. Stop and classify the troublesome material again rather than forcing it into the current document.

When a request contains more than one quadrant, separate the needs into linked documents. Choose one primary need for the current document and move each remaining need to its corresponding type.
